Types of poems

Alliterationsame starting consonant sound: Seven silly stars sparkle.
Acrosticfirst letters of the lines spell a word
Quatrain4 lines (may rhyme)
Cinquain5 lines: 1 noun · 2 adjectives · 3 -ing words · 4-word phrase · 1 related word

Parts of speech: noun = person/place/thing/idea · adjective = describes a noun · verb = action or state. The bright (adj) rocket (noun) flies (verb).

Unit 5 · Figurative language, rhyme & rhythm
Similecompares using like / as — The Moon is like a pearl.
Metaphorsays one thing IS another — The classroom is a zoo.
Personificationhuman action for a nonhuman thing — The wind whispered.
Hyperbolevery big exaggeration — I waited a thousand years!

Rhyme = same/similar ending sounds (night/light). Rhythm = pattern of strong and weak beats; read aloud to hear it.

How to read a poem: 1) topic/theme 2) sensory details 3) rhyme & rhythm 4) figurative language 5) explain the effect on the reader.
Unit 6 · Tuck Everlasting — Setting & characters

Setting: late 1800s, in and near the town of Treegap — Winnie’s house, the wood with the magical spring, the Tuck home near a pond.

Winnie Fostercurious 10-year-old who wants freedom; must make a life-changing choice
Jesse Tuckforever seventeen; hopes Winnie will live forever too
Miles Tuckolder son; lost his wife and children because he doesn’t age
Mae Tuckmother; protects the family and the secret
Angus Tuckfather; explains why life needs change and an ending
Man in the Yellow Suitstranger who wants to sell the magic water
The Constableinvestigates Winnie’s disappearance; arrests Mae
The FostersWinnie’s parents and grandmother; want to keep her safe
The ToadWinnie pours the spring water on it near the end
Unit 6 · Plot, conflicts & themes

Beginning: Winnie feels protected but trapped. She goes into the wood, meets Jesse at the spring; Mae and Miles take her to the Tuck home to explain the secret.

Middle: The Tucks explain the spring; Angus warns about living forever. The man in the yellow suit learns the secret to sell the water. He threatens Winnie, Mae strikes him, he dies, and Mae is arrested.

Ending: Mae may be executed (which would reveal she can’t die). Winnie takes Mae’s place in the jail cell so the Tucks can free her. Jesse gives Winnie a bottle of spring water, but she pours it on a toad. Years later, Angus and Mae return and learn Winnie grew old and died — she chose a normal human life.

Person vs. selfWinnie decides about freedom, friendship, eternal life
Person vs. personTucks vs. the man in the yellow suit
Person vs. society/rulesWinnie breaks rules to help Mae escape
Person vs. nature/timeTucks can’t join the cycle of growing old and dying

Themes (a message, not one word): life and death · time and change · freedom and responsibility · family and love. Example: “Life has meaning because it changes and ends.” Evidence: Angus describes life as a moving wheel; Winnie grows old instead of drinking the water.
